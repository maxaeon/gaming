Proof Lab

Logical derivations are structured proofs where each step follows logically from previous steps using inference rules.

Given: "If P then Q" (P→Q) and "P", you can conclude: Q
Given: "If P then Q" (P→Q) and "¬Q", you can conclude: ¬P
Given: "P→Q" and "Q→R", you can conclude: P→R
Given: "P∨Q" and "¬P", you can conclude: Q

Beyond Propositional Logic

Predicate logic analyzes the structure within statements using quantifiers, helping us express arguments that propositional logic cannot capture. Modal logic introduces notions like necessity and possibility. Categorical logic, though limited, fits into this richer framework.

All humans are mortal. ∀x(Hx → Mx)
Socrates is human. Hs
Therefore, Socrates is mortal. Ms

Modal Examples:

Necessarily, bachelors are unmarried. □∀x(Bx → ¬Mx)
Possibly someone wins the lottery. ◇∃x(Lx)

Categorical Example:

All mammals are animals. ∀x(Mx → Ax)
