Thinker's Arcade

Argument Reconstruction Activity

This activity lets you practice identifying premises and conclusions, distinguishing moral from nonmoral premises, and evaluating arguments. It follows Lewis Vaughn's guideline that moral arguments typically include at least one moral and one nonmoral premise.

Summary

This activity introduces several tools for evaluating arguments. A premise gives a reason to accept a conclusion, and moral arguments usually combine at least one moral premise with a nonmoral one.

A deductive argument aims for certainty—it is valid if the conclusion must be true whenever the premises are true and sound when those premises are in fact true. An inductive argument offers only probability; it is strong when the premises make the conclusion likely and cogent when those premises are also true.

Identifying hidden or missing premises helps clarify whether an argument succeeds according to these standards.
