Draft Development

In this activity, we'll show how to develop a philosophy draft step-by-step. We'll use Kant’s theory of moral absolutism as our example. Afterward, you’ll get a customizable template for your own writing projects.

Example Thesis

Kant’s categorical imperative is overly rigid, failing to account for morally complex scenarios.

A good thesis clearly states your argument and sets the stage for your paper.

Body Paragraph 1

Kant’s categorical imperative demands universal moral rules without exceptions.

Refer to Kant’s Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals for the formulation of the categorical imperative.

Body Paragraph 2

However, Kant’s insistence on absolute truthfulness can lead to ethically troubling outcomes.

Consider the Nazi-at-the-door scenario discussed in many textbooks.

Body Paragraph 3

Morally effective decisions often require flexibility and attention to consequences.

For example, Aristotle’s notion of practical wisdom emphasizes context.

Conclusion

Therefore, Kant’s moral absolutism, while theoretically consistent, struggles with complex moral realities.

References

  • Kant, Immanuel. Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals.
  • Course Textbook on Ethics.
